The story

Most firms start counting
at the launch. We start at one.

Most software firms start counting at the launch. Before that there is the first conversation, the scoping, the architecture, the hosting plan — the part nobody is watching, and the part that decides whether the thing works at all.

We start at one.

99 is the last number you need to count to. It is the point where a system stops being a project and starts being infrastructure — and it is the point where most firms leave. We are not arriving there. We were there at one, at forty, at ninety.

One to ninety-nine: scoping, engineering, hosting, maintenance, enhancement. A small senior team, so nothing is thrown over a wall — there is no wall. The people who scope your work are the people who build it, host it and keep it alive. One accountable name for the whole count.

And when a count ends, the next one starts. Enhancements are not an afterthought or an upsell — they are the next count, and they are part of the job we signed up for.

We never claim 100. Not because we are incomplete, but because there is always one more count worth starting.

For companies that want to digitalise how they actually work — and stay ahead of the ones that won't — 99 is a small senior team that owns the whole count: scoping, software, hosting, maintenance and enhancement, under one accountable name, from the first conversation to the last number — and the next one after it.

One naming note — a direction we rejected

The first draft of this story leaned on an alphabetic frame — the idea of covering the whole range from the first letter to the last. It is out. AtoZ Electronics is an established Maltese retailer with shops in Attard and PAVI Qormi; in this market that territory is theirs, and a software firm borrowing it reads as a reseller, which is the opposite of the positioning.

The replacement, “One to ninety-nine”, claims the same coverage without the collision, ties the claim to the company's own name instead of a borrowed idiom, and fixes a second problem: the earlier wording made the offer sound like post-launch support, when it is the whole lifecycle.

Final directions

Choose one.
02 and 03 answer the same question — take one of them.

All four read as “ninety-nine” with no wordmark and survive one colour. They differ in what they ask the rest of the identity to do.

01

The Sign

Idea-led · typographic sign

The idea
A closing quotation mark is a 99. It is the sign that ends the statement.
Why it carries the story
The name is the symbol, so a lockup has no redundant “9 + 99”. Best small-size behaviour of the three: it still reads as punctuation at 16 px.
Honest risk
Quotation marks are common glyphs. Ownership comes from the drawn tail and consistent use.
Decision
Pick it if you want the name itself to be the symbol.

02

The Wordmark

Craft-led · custom wordmark

The idea
Two drawn numerals: round bowls, decisive swept tails, flat-cut terminals.
Why it carries the story
The plainest reading of the name, and the warmest. Nothing to decode, so it survives being printed small on an invoice footer.
Honest risk
Quiet. It asks the rest of the identity to carry the personality.
Decision
Pick it if you want quiet authority and zero misreading.

03

Sign-off

Craft-led · underlined wordmark

The idea
The last stroke keeps going and underlines both numerals: the work is underlined, then signed off.
Why it carries the story
The underline is the only element carrying meaning rather than decoration — it reads as a signature line and gives the wordmark a baseline to sit on in dark UI.
Honest risk
An underline can also read as a hyperlink or as emphasis. It competes directly with 02 for the same role — pick one of the two, not both.
Decision
Pick it if you want the wordmark to end on a line rather than on a curve.

04

The Seal

Strategy-led · negative-space emblem

The idea
Two negative-space 9s carved through one solid, chamfered monolith.
Why it carries the story
Reads as finished and sealed — the A-to-Z promise as a single mass. Works as an app icon, avatar or stamp with no extra container.
Honest risk
Dense. The channels thin below 24 px, so it needs a small-size cut; reversed weight needs a nudge.
Decision
Pick it if you want a mark that doubles as an icon and a stamp.
