01 — The story

Most firms start counting at the launch.
We start at one.

Before the launch there is the first conversation, the scoping, the architecture, the hosting plan — the part nobody is watching, and the part that decides whether the thing works at all.

99 is the last number you need to count to. It is the point where a system stops being a project and starts being infrastructure — and it is the point where most firms leave. We are not arriving there. We were there at one, at forty, at ninety.

One to ninety-nine: scoping, software, hosting, improvement and growth. A small senior team, so nothing is thrown over a wall — there is no wall. One accountable name for the whole count.

And when a count ends, the next one starts. Continual improvement is not an upsell; it is the next count.

We never claim 100. Not because we are incomplete, but because there is always one more count worth starting.

For companies that want to digitalise how they actually work — and stay ahead of the ones that won't — 99 is a small senior team that builds solutions right and improves them continually: scoping, software, hosting, improvement and growth, under one accountable name.

02 — Logo directions

Five directions.
Pick one.

Choose between the written name, ninetynine, and the compact numeral-led marks. Each works in one colour and stands alone—no extra symbol or repeated “99”. The written name is especially clear in website headers and business documents; use a compact mark for tiny favicon sizes.

Marks are drawn as filled vector paths — no font is used inside any logo, so nothing reflows on a machine without the typeface.

03 — Package

Colour and type travel together.

A package is a palette plus a type pairing, sold as one decision. That is not a gimmick — it is because two things are true at once.

1 · A hard constraint. An accent has to clear contrast on the ground it sits on. Forge's brass accent measures 2.35:1 on a light ground — it fails even the large-text threshold. So each package carries two accent tokens: one for Ink, one for Paper, and both are measured rather than estimated.
2 · A soft constraint. The typeface sets the voice — grotesk reads neutral and confident, serif reads editorial and premium, mono reads engineering-forward. A palette that disagrees with the typeface makes the brand look like two companies. These three pairings are opinionated on purpose.

08 — Domain

The name is short. The domains are not.

Checked against RDAP: 11 of the candidates are unregistered and 8 are taken. Availability moves hourly — confirm at a registrar before you rely on any of these.

Recommendation: hold 99.systems as the primary and ninetynine.mt defensively for the local market. A bare numeral is hard to protect as a trademark, so the domain and the wordmark do more of the work than usual — budget for a professional clearance search on the name before launch.
