# 99 — Brand strategy

*Draft v1. Assumes a small senior team of 5–20; buyer is any company that wants to digitise how it actually works.
Correct anything that is wrong — this is the document everything else hangs from.*

---

## 1. The one line

> **A to Z. Then 99.**

## 2. The story

Every project has an **A**: a first conversation, a first line of code, a process that still runs on paper.

Most firms are excellent at the A. They are brilliant at the **Z** — the launch, the demo, the invoice. What they
are not good at is *everything after Z*: the hosting that has to stay up, the dependency that needs upgrading in
eighteen months, the enhancement the business needs the moment it changes shape.

**99 exists for after Z.**

We are a small senior team, so nothing is thrown over a wall — there is no wall. The people who scope your work are
the people who build it, host it, and keep it alive. Software, hosting, maintenance, enhancements: one accountable
name from the first letter to the last number.

And the name? **99 is the last number you need to count to.** You count to 99, then you stop counting and start using
the thing. It is the point where a project stops being a project.

We never claim 100 — not because we are incomplete, but because there is always one more improvement worth making,
and that is precisely the work we are signing up for.

## 3. Positioning statement

> For companies that want to digitalise how they actually work — and stay ahead of the ones that won't — **99** is the
> small senior team that takes a process from A to Z and then keeps it running: software, hosting, maintenance and
> enhancements, under one accountable name.

## 4. Three pillars

| Pillar | Means | Shows up as |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineered** | Exactness is the argument. No approximations, no leftover artefacts. | Drawn geometry, real numbers, hairline rules, no decoration |
| **Whole** | A to Z, and after Z. One team, one contract, one name. | Full lifecycle in the copy; nothing handed off |
| **Human** | Warm and plain-spoken. Technically precise never means cold. | Warm palette, sentence case, no jargon |

## 5. Voice

**Do:** short sentences. Specific quantities. Name the awkward thing (the 2 a.m. page, the migration nobody wanted).
Say "we". Admit the limits of the trade.

**Don't:** *cutting-edge, synergy, digital transformation journey, leverage, robust, seamless, world-class,
passionate about technology.* Never say "99 % complete" — the number means finish, not near-finish.

## 6. Palette — proposal v1

Taken from the website direction you shared, then tightened. The category (checked against 99x, Thoughtworks,
8th Light, Made Tech) is either cool neutrals with one accent, or a bright multi-accent system. **Warm near-black
plus one ember accent plus warm cream is unoccupied.**

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Ink** | `#120C0A` | Default ground. Warm black — never pure `#000` |
| **Ember** | `#D8440B` | The single hot accent. Hero blocks, one CTA, the 99 in a lockup |
| **Deep Ember** | `#6E1F06` | Pressed / hover; print-safe dark of the accent |
| **Sand** | `#E9CBA9` | Warm light surface; secondary text on Ink |
| **Paper** | `#FAF5EE` | Light-mode ground |
| **Slate** | `#8A8078` | Tertiary text, dividers, disabled |

Rules: Ember never carries body text. Ink and Paper are the only grounds. One accent per screen.
Measured contrast (WCAG): Paper on Ink **17.9:1**, Sand on Ink **12.5:1**, Slate on Ink 5.0:1,
Ember on Ink **4.4:1**, Ember on Paper **4.1:1**. Ember therefore never sets body text — large text (≥24 px,
or ≥18.7 px bold) and non-text UI only, on either ground. Sand is the safe secondary text colour.

## 7. Typography

- **Display / UI:** Inter Tight (OFL) — neutral grotesk, tight tracking at size, sentence case.
- **Numerals, micro-labels, stats:** JetBrains Mono (OFL) — uppercase, `0.16em` tracking, the "engineered" tell.
- The two-tone headline device from your reference (first clause bright, continuation in Slate) is on-brand: it reads
  as *the fact, then the detail*.
- **No font is used in any logo** — all marks are drawn as filled paths.

## 8. The three final logos

Two things the name has to survive: it is read at 16 px in a browser tab, and it is said out loud on a call.
All three read as "ninety-nine" without a wordmark; all three are single-colour safe.

| | Mark | Role | Pick it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01** | **The Sign** — closing quotation mark | Idea-led, most distinctive | you want the name itself to be the symbol, and the strongest small-size behaviour |
| **02** | **The Wordmark** — cut & curve numerals | Craft-led, warmest | you want quiet authority and a mark that cannot be misread |
| **03** | **The Seal** — negative-space monolith | Strategy-led, most solid | you want a mark that doubles as an app icon and a stamp |

Open items before launch: professional trademark search (name is close to 99x and 99designs), print/Pantone proofing,
and a reversed-weight optical pass.
