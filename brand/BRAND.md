# 99 — Brand strategy

*Draft v2. Corrected: the story no longer implies a maintenance-only shop, and the "A to Z" frame is gone
(it collides with a Maltese retailer). Assumes a small senior team of 5–20.*

---

## 1. The one line

> **One to ninety-nine.**

Short form for tight lockups: **The whole count.**

## 2. The story

Most software firms start counting at the launch. Before that there is the first conversation, the scoping, the
architecture, the hosting plan — the part nobody is watching, and the part that decides whether the thing works at
all.

**We start at one.**

**99 is the last number you need to count to.** It is the point where a system stops being a project and starts
being infrastructure — and it is the point where most firms leave. We are not arriving there. We were there at one,
at forty, at ninety.

One to ninety-nine: scoping, engineering, hosting, maintenance, enhancement. A small senior team, so nothing is
thrown over a wall — there is no wall. The people who scope your work are the people who build it, host it and keep
it alive. One accountable name for the whole count.

And when a count ends, the next one starts. Enhancements are not an afterthought or an upsell — they are the next
count, and they are part of the job we signed up for.

We never claim 100. Not because we are incomplete, but because there is always one more count worth starting.

### Why it is not a maintenance story

The first draft said "99 exists for after Z", which made the offer sound like post-launch support with the build
somewhere else. That is wrong and it was cut. The story now runs **from the first conversation**, and 99 is the
endpoint of a span the company owns end to end — not the part left over at the end.

## 3. Rejected: the alphabetic "A to Z" frame

The original frame was A→Z. It has been dropped deliberately:

- **AtoZ Electronics** ([atoz.com.mt](https://atoz.com.mt/)) is an established Maltese retailer with shops in Attard
  and PAVI Qormi. In this market the phrase is theirs, and a software firm borrowing it reads as a reseller — the
  opposite of the positioning.
- "After Z" also implied maintenance-only, which contradicts the full-lifecycle offer.

**"One to ninety-nine"** does the same job and more: it claims the whole span, it ties the claim to the company's own
name instead of a borrowed idiom, and it keeps the completion idea intact.

If the phrase is ever used as a registered slogan or in an ad campaign, check its availability in Malta first.

## 4. Positioning statement

> For companies that want to digitalise how they actually work — and stay ahead of the ones that won't — **99** is a
> small senior team that owns the *whole count*: scoping, software, hosting, maintenance and enhancement, under one
> accountable name, from the first conversation to the last number — and the next one after it.

## 5. Three pillars

| Pillar | Means | Shows up as |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineered** | Exactness is the argument. No approximations, no leftover artefacts. | Drawn geometry, real numbers, hairline rules, no decoration |
| **Whole count** | Scope to enhancement, build to hosting. One team, one contract, one name. | Full lifecycle in the copy; nothing handed off, nothing left at the launch |
| **Human** | Warm and plain-spoken. Precise never means cold. | Warm palette, sentence case, no jargon |

## 6. Voice

**Do:** short sentences. Specific quantities. Name the awkward thing — the 2 a.m. page, the migration nobody wanted,
the dependency that expires in eighteen months. Say "we". Admit the limits of the trade.

**Don't:** *cutting-edge, synergy, digital transformation journey, leverage, robust, seamless, world-class, passionate
about technology, one-stop shop.* Never say "99 % complete" — the number means finish, not near-finish.

## 7. Palette — v1

Checked against 99x, Thoughtworks, 8th Light and Made Tech: the category is either cool neutrals with one accent, or a
bright multi-accent spectrum. **Warm near-black + one ember accent + warm cream is unoccupied.**

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Ink** | `#120C0A` | Default ground. Warm black — never pure `#000` |
| **Ember** | `#D8440B` | The single hot accent. Hero blocks, one CTA, the 99 in a lockup |
| **Deep Ember** | `#6E1F06` | Pressed / hover; print-safe dark of the accent |
| **Sand** | `#E9CBA9` | Warm light surface; **the safe secondary text colour on Ink** |
| **Paper** | `#FAF5EE` | Light-mode ground |
| **Slate** | `#8A8078` | Tertiary text, dividers, disabled |

Rules: Ember never carries body text. Ink and Paper are the only grounds. One accent per screen.

**Measured contrast (WCAG):** Paper on Ink **17.9:1** · Sand on Ink **12.5:1** · Slate on Ink 5.0:1 ·
Ember on Ink **4.4:1** · Ember on Paper **4.1:1**. Ember is therefore large text (≥24 px, or ≥18.7 px bold) and
non-text UI only, on either ground.

## 8. Typography

- **Display / UI:** Inter Tight (OFL) — neutral grotesk, tight tracking at size, sentence case.
- **Numerals, micro-labels, stats:** JetBrains Mono (OFL) — uppercase, `0.16em` tracking, the "engineered" tell.
- The two-tone headline device from your reference (first clause bright, continuation in Slate) is on-brand: it reads
  as *the fact, then the detail*.
- **No font is used in any logo** — every mark is drawn as filled paths.

## 9. The final logos

All four read as "ninety-nine" with no wordmark and survive one colour. **02 and 03 answer the same question — pick
one of those two, not both.**

| # | Mark | Role | Pick it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01** | **The Sign** — closing quotation mark | Idea-led, typographic | you want the name and the symbol to be the same object; strongest at 16 px |
| **02** | **The Wordmark** — cut & curve numerals | Craft-led, warmest | you want quiet authority and zero misreading |
| **03** | **Sign-off** — underlined wordmark | Craft-led, structured | you want the wordmark to end on a line, not a curve |
| **04** | **The Seal** — negative-space monolith | Strategy-led, emblem | you want a mark that doubles as an app icon and a stamp |

Open items before launch: professional trademark search (the name is close to 99x and 99designs, and a bare numeral
is hard to protect), print/Pantone proofing, a reversed-weight optical pass, and a small-size cut for 04.
